# Features, stage by stage

Bazaar was built in numbered stages, each a complete feature with its tests, committed and deployed on its own
(`git log --grep "Stage N"` shows one). Ideas not built yet are in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md).

1. **Catalog**: categories, search, filters (brand, price, rating, availability), sorting, pagination, product pages.
2. **Accounts, cart and checkout**: sign up and log in, a cart kept in the browser (no login needed to shop), checkout with Cash on Delivery, order history, and cancelling an order that hasn't shipped. Delivery is free from ₹499, otherwise ₹40.

3. **Admin panel** (`/admin`, admins only): orders (search, filter by status, mark shipped → delivered, cancel with stock returned), products (add and edit with photo uploads, quick stock edits, hide/show), and categories (add, rename, reorder, delete when empty).

4. **Online payments** with Razorpay in **test mode**: UPI, cards, netbanking and wallets next to Cash on Delivery. Stock is held for 30 minutes while the customer pays; unpaid orders then expire. Payments are verified on the server (signature + Razorpay API, and a webhook as backup); cancelling a paid order refunds it automatically.

5. **Reviews and wishlist**: customers rate and review products they've received (one review each, which they can edit or delete); new reviews are added to the sample ratings. Admins can hide a review, which also takes it out of the rating. A wishlist kept in the account, with a Wishlist page next to My orders.

6. **Order emails** through [Brevo](https://www.brevo.com): the customer hears when an order is confirmed (for online orders, once paid), shipped, delivered (with links to review each item) and cancelled (saying whether a refund was started). Without a Brevo key, emails are printed in the server log instead.

7. **Password reset by email**: "Forgot password?" on the login page emails a one-time link (valid for 1 hour); choosing a new password logs you out everywhere else.

8. **Better search**: words also match category names and product highlights, plurals and a few synonyms work ("phones" finds the Mobiles category), whole words only ("book" doesn't find Chromebooks), and results are ranked by how well they match, then popularity.

9. **Login protection**: after 5 wrong passwords, login for that email pauses for 15 minutes (a password reset ends it sooner), and a reset can't reuse the current password.

10. **Account page** (`/account`): change your name, edit or remove the saved address checkout fills in, and change your password (the current one is needed, it can't be reused, and other devices are logged out).

11. **Back-in-stock alerts**: on a sold-out product, logged-in customers click "Email me when it's back" and get one email when it's in stock again (checked every minute, however the stock came back).

12. **Search suggestions**: typing in the header's search box shows matching categories and the best 6 products (ranked like search), plus "See all N results"; arrow keys, Enter and Escape work too.

13. **Coupon codes**: admins create codes (percentage off with an optional cap, an amount off, or free delivery; optional minimum order, last day and total uses) under Admin → Coupons, and switch them on or off. Customers apply one at checkout; each customer can use a code once, and cancelling the order gives the use back.

14. **Sales dashboard**: the admin panel opens on a dashboard for the last 7, 30 or 90 days: revenue, orders, average order and discounts given (compared with the period before), revenue per day as a chart (also as a table), best sellers, orders waiting to ship, and products running low.

15. **New-order alerts**: admins get an email for every new order (Cash on Delivery straight away, online orders once paid) with the customer, items, total, payment and delivery city, and a link to the order in the admin panel. They go to every admin account, or to `ADMIN_EMAIL` if it's set.

16. **Unused uploads cleaned up**: photos uploaded in the admin panel but never saved to a product (or replaced since) are deleted once they're a day old, checked at startup and every hour, so they don't fill the free database. Photos that past orders show are kept.

17. **Login limit per network**: besides the 5-per-email lock, more than 20 wrong passwords from one network (an IP address, or an IPv6 /64) within 15 minutes pause login from that network, whichever emails were tried, so nobody can try one common password on many accounts. Generous on purpose: mobile networks put many people behind one address. On Render the visitor's address comes from the `CF-Connecting-IP` header (`CLIENT_IP_HEADER` in `render.yaml`).

18. **Change email**: on the account page, enter the new address and your password; a link (valid 24 hours) goes to the new address, and the email changes only once it's opened (on any device). The old address is then told, and reset links sent to it stop working. A waiting change shows on the account page and can be cancelled.

19. **Printable invoices**: placed, shipped and delivered orders have an Invoice button (for the customer on their order page, for admins on the admin order page) that opens a bill with the order number and date, billing address and email, items, delivery, coupon, total and how it's paid. "Print or save as PDF" prints just the invoice, black on white.

20. **Export orders**: on the admin dashboard, choose two dates (India time, both included) and optionally a status, and download a CSV for Excel or Google Sheets: one row per order with its number, date and time, status, customer and email, items, amounts in rupees (items, delivery, discount, coupon, total), payment and delivery city, state and PIN code.

21. **Product variants**: a product can come in one kind of option (sizes, colours…), each with its own price, MRP and stock. Customers pick one on the product page (sold-out ones are crossed out); listings say "from ₹…" when prices differ, and the cart, checkout, orders, invoices and exports name the variant ("Stride Running Shoes (Size: UK 8)"). Admins switch variants on in the product form and edit them in a table. The demo's Stride Running Shoes and Urbane Women Kurta Set have sizes.

22. **Photos per variant**: in the product form, tick which of the product's photos belong to each variant; choosing that variant in the store shows only its photos, and its cart and order lines use its first one. With none ticked, a variant shows all the product's photos. The demo's Ziva Women Handbag has a Black and a Tan.

23. **Alerts per size**: a sold-out size or colour stays choosable (crossed out) while others are in stock; choosing it offers "Email me when UK 10's back", and the email names the size. A customer can watch several sizes of one product; an alert for a size an admin deletes is dropped.

24. **Two options**: a product can have two kinds of option, e.g. Colour and Waist, each combination with its own price, stock and photos (combinations not sold are simply left out). In the store, the second row of choices follows the first (a waist not made in the chosen colour can't be picked) and choosing just a colour already shows its photos; lines read "Urbane Men Slim Fit Jeans (Colour: Black, Waist: 32)". In the admin form, "Add a second option" adds a column, and "Add missing combinations" fills in the grid. The demo's jeans come in Blue and Black.

25. **Database backups**: `npm run backup` saves the Atlas database to a dated folder on your computer (outside the project), and `npm run restore` puts one back (see [Backups](#backups)).

26. **Tracking numbers**: when marking an order shipped, the admin can enter the courier, the tracking number and the courier's tracking link (pasted from its site); they can be added or corrected later. The customer sees them on the order page, with a "Track your parcel" link, and in the shipped email; the order export has them too.

27. **Link previews**: a product link shared on WhatsApp, Instagram, Facebook or X shows a card with its name and price ("from ₹…" for variants at different prices), brand and highlights, and its first uploaded photo; every other page, and products with only the generated sample pictures (previews can't show SVG), use the store's own Bazaar image. The browser tab shows the product's name too.

28. **Returns**: for 7 days after delivery, customers can ask to return some items of an order (how many of each, a reason and a note); the refund is those items' price less their share of any coupon, without the delivery. Admins are emailed and decide in Admin → Returns: approving (once the items are back) puts the stock back and refunds online payments through Razorpay; for Cash on Delivery the admin pays the customer and marks it refunded. Declining says why. The customer is emailed either way and sees the status on the order page.

29. **Delivery PIN check**: product pages have "Check delivery": enter a PIN code to see whether it's delivered there and by when (e.g. "by Sat, 24 Oct – Sun, 25 Oct"); the saved address's PIN, or the last one checked, is filled in. In Admin → Delivery, areas by the first digits of the PIN code (560 = Bengaluru, 1–2 days; 79 = not delivered; the longest match wins) and a rule for everywhere else (3–7 days until set). Checkout refuses addresses the store doesn't deliver to.

30. **Two-step login**: on the account page, anyone can turn on login codes from an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Authy…): confirm the password, scan the QR code or type the key, enter a code, and save the 8 one-time recovery codes. Logging in then asks for a code after the password (5 wrong codes per try; each code works once), a password reset link no longer logs in by itself, and turning it off needs the password and a code. The admin panel reminds admins without it. Lost the phone and the recovery codes: `npm run reset-two-step -- <email>` (database credentials needed).

31. **Returns in the sales figures**: the dashboard's revenue is net of refunds from approved returns ("₹… in sales − ₹… refunded"), compared with the period before the same way; a refund counts on the day of the order it came from. The chart's tooltip and table show each day's refunds, best sellers count items kept (sold less returned), and the order export has Refunded (₹) and Net total (₹) columns.
32. **Show up on Google**: `/robots.txt` keeps the admin panel, account, cart, checkout and order pages out of search engines and points to `/sitemap.xml`, which lists the home page, each category and every shown product (with when it last changed). Product pages carry schema.org data (name, brand, price in rupees or a price range for variants, in stock or not, rating) so Google can show price and stars in results, and a canonical address.
33. **Similar products and recently viewed**: each product page shows up to 8 similar products (same category and brand first, in stock before sold out) and, like the home page, the products this browser viewed lately (up to 10, newest first, with a Clear button). Recently viewed is kept only in the browser, like the cart; prices and stock always come from the server.
34. **Daily low-stock email**: each morning (from 9:00 India time, or when the free plan next wakes up that day) the admins get one email listing the products in the store with 5 or fewer left, sold out first, naming the sizes and colours that are low, with a link to the admin panel's low-stock list. Nothing is sent on a day when nothing is low. It goes to `ADMIN_EMAIL`, or every admin account, like new-order alerts, and can be switched off (and on) for everyone with a tick box under the dashboard's low-stock list.
35. **Products from a spreadsheet**: Admin → Products → Import / export CSV downloads every product as a CSV (a row per size or colour below products that have them) to edit in Excel or Google Sheets. Uploading it shows a preview first: new products, each change ("Price ₹349 → ₹299") and rows with problems; saving writes every row or none, and is refused if the store changed since the preview. A row without a slug adds a product (with a placeholder photo); empty cells keep what's there; prices are rupees. Photos and new sizes stay in the product form.
36. **Activity log**: Admin → Activity lists every change made in the admin panel, newest first: who, when, what (with a link) and each change ("Price: ₹349 → ₹299"), for products (and CSV imports), categories, orders, returns, reviews, coupons, delivery areas and the low-stock email switch, plus `npm run make-admin` and `npm run reset-two-step` (shown as the command line). Filter by area or person. Entries are kept for a year and can't be edited or deleted.
37. **Questions on products**: logged-in customers ask a question on a product page (up to 500 characters, 5 an hour); until it's answered only they see it ("waiting for an answer") and can delete it. Admins answer, edit answers and hide questions in Admin → Questions (waiting ones oldest first, and counted under the dashboard's "Needs attention"); answered questions show on the product page for everyone, and the customer is emailed the first answer.
38. **GST invoices**: Admin → GST takes the store's GSTIN (checked, including its check character and state code), registered name, address and state, a default rate and each category's rate (0, 0.25, 3, 5, 18 or 40%) and HSN code. While it's on, each new order remembers them, and its invoice becomes a tax invoice: seller and GSTIN, place of supply, each line's taxable value, rate and tax (CGST + SGST within the store's state, IGST otherwise), totals and tax by rate, in rupees and paise. Prices include GST, so what customers pay doesn't change; coupons lower the taxable values and delivery is taxed at the order's highest rate. Orders placed before (or with GST off) keep their plain invoice. Addresses now take a state or union territory from a list of suggestions ("tamilnadu" is saved as Tamil Nadu).
39. **Faster first visit**: shoppers no longer download the admin panel, invoices, return requests, the account page or the QR code library; each loads the first time it's opened (the main file went from 437 KB to 325 KB, about 100 KB compressed). Built files are cached by browsers for a year (their names change with every build), and a tab left open across a deploy reloads once to get the new build instead of breaking.
40. **Price-drop emails**: the wishlist remembers each product's price when saved; when a product on it, shown and in stock, becomes at least 5% cheaper than the price the customer last saw, they get one email listing everything that dropped (checked every minute, like back-in-stock alerts). Rises and falls back don't count again. Customers switch them off or on under Email updates on the account page; the wishlist page says which.
41. **Cart saved to the account**: a logged-in customer's cart is saved in their account and follows them between devices (loaded on login and when they come back to the tab). A guest's cart joins the account's on login; logging out empties the browser's cart, which stays in the account. A cart left alone for a day (and less than a week) gets one reminder email with today's prices, not if they've ordered since; customers switch these off under Email updates.
42. **Packing slips and shipping in bulk**: in Admin → Orders, tick orders (or all on the page) to print packing slips (one page per order: ship-to address and phone, items with quantities and a box to tick, no prices, and the cash to collect for Cash on Delivery) or to mark them all shipped at once (each customer emailed and each order in the activity log as if shipped one by one; orders that can't be shipped are listed with the reason). Up to 50 at a time.
43. **Private notes on orders**: admins add notes to an order (who and when shown, up to 1000 characters, deletable), which customers never see in their order, emails or invoices. A note can also be marked to print on the packing slip ("deliver after 6 pm"); the orders list shows how many notes an order has, and adding or deleting one goes in the activity log.
44. **Photos in reviews**: buyers add up to 3 photos to a review. The browser shrinks them (at most 1600 px, under 1 MB, as JPEG) before uploading, and the server removes where and when a photo was taken (EXIF/GPS, text) from JPEG and PNG files. Photos show as thumbnails on the product page and in Admin → Reviews; hiding a review hides them too. Uploads are limited to 20 an hour per customer, and ones never used in a review are deleted after a day.
45. **GST in the order export**: the order CSV has Place of supply, Taxable value, CGST, SGST and IGST columns for orders placed while GST invoices were on, and a second report, GST summary, adds up sold orders (placed, shipped, delivered) by place of supply and rate (orders, taxable value, CGST, SGST, IGST, total tax), as B2C sales are reported in the GST return. Credit notes for returns are taken off since stage 50.
46. **Accessibility check**: the browser tests run axe (`@axe-core/playwright`) on every page, shop and admin, against WCAG 2.1 A and AA, in the light theme and on the most colourful pages in the dark one, so CI fails if a page loses contrast, a label or a name. The first run found and fixed: grey text, rating badges, status badges and the dark invoice title below 4.5:1 contrast, links in sentences that only differed by colour (now underlined), and picture links with no name in the cart, order and wishlist.
47. **Storage meter**: the admin dashboard shows how much of the database's space is used (Atlas's free tier: 512 MB, or `STORAGE_LIMIT_MB`), with a bar that turns orange at 80% and red at 95%, and the biggest users (photos, products, orders…). Admins are emailed when it passes 80% and 95%, then at most weekly while it stays there. Measured as data plus indexes before compression, so it errs on the early side.
48. **"Did you mean…?"**: a search that finds nothing suggests the closest words the store knows (from shown products' names, brands, highlights and category names), for example "electirc kettel" → "electric kettle" (one typo in short words, two in longer ones), only when the corrected search finds something with the same filters, and says how many.
49. **Buy again**: a button on any past order (not one still awaiting payment) puts its items back in the cart at today's prices, as many as were bought or as are left; the cart then says what was added and what wasn't (no longer sold, out of stock, now needs a size or colour, or a full cart). Items already in the cart keep the larger quantity.
50. **Credit notes for returns**: approving a return of an order placed with GST on issues a GST credit note (its own series: CN-1, CN-2, …), with the returned items' refund split into taxable value and CGST/SGST or IGST at the invoice's rates. Customers and admins open it from the order page and print it like the invoice, and the GST summary takes credit notes off by the date they were issued, so it shows sales net of returns.
51. **Write for me** (optional, costs a little per use): with an Anthropic API key, the admin product form gets a button that asks Claude to draft the description and highlights from the title, brand, category, the highlights typed so far and the first uploaded photo. The admin checks and edits the draft before saving; nothing is saved by the button. Without a key the button isn't shown. 30 drafts per admin per hour.
52. **Error reports from the browser**: when a page fails to load or crashes for a visitor, the browser tells the store, and Admin → Errors lists each problem once with how often it happened, when, on which pages, in which browser, and the details a developer needs (stack). Admins mark a problem fixed; it opens again if it happens again. The dashboard says how many are open. At most 30 reports an hour per network, 500 different problems kept, each for 90 days after its last report.
53. **New-browser login alerts for admins**: when an admin account logs in from a browser it hasn't used before (another device, another browser, or after clearing cookies), that admin is emailed with the time, the browser ("Chrome on Windows") and the IP address, and what to do if it wasn't them; the activity log notes it too. Admins' browsers are recognised by a long-lived cookie (customers don't get one); a browser unused for a year counts as new again. A new IP address alone doesn't alert, as phones change address all the time. With two-step login, the alert comes once the code is accepted.
54. **Address book**: customers keep up to 10 delivery addresses (home, office, parents…), each with an optional label, on the account page: add, edit, remove and make one the default. Checkout offers them as choices with the default picked, or a new address with "Save it to my addresses" ticked; a saved address is never changed by ordering. The single saved address from before becomes the first entry automatically.
55. **Frequently bought together**: a product page shows up to 6 products other customers bought in the same orders, above "Similar products". A product appears only once at least two different customers bought it with this one, so no one's order can be seen through it; only real sales count (not unpaid or cancelled orders), and hidden or sold-out products are left out. Until then the row isn't shown.
56. **AI answer drafts** (optional, costs a little per use, same key as Write for me): in Admin → Questions, "Draft an answer" asks Claude for a reply from the product's details and the store's earlier answers about it, and puts it in the answer box for the admin to check, edit and send. When the details don't settle the question (say, a warranty that isn't mentioned), it says so instead of guessing. Nothing is sent or saved by the button.
57. **Explain this error** (optional, same key): on Admin → Errors, a button asks Claude what a reported crash or failed page load most likely means, in plain words, where in the code to look first, and whether it can stop people buying. The explanation is kept with the report, so looking again costs nothing. Links are removed from it, since anyone could have sent the report. The browser build now keeps component names, so every report names the real component (about 10 KB more to download, gzipped).
58. **Weekly note for admins** (optional, same key, one call a week): each Monday from 9 am India time, admins get an email about last week (Monday to Sunday): a few paragraphs Claude writes from the week's figures (sales and orders against the week before, best sellers, cancellations, new customers, returns, reviews, and what needs attention now), with the figures themselves below so they can be checked. If Claude can't write it, the figures go alone. Switch it off on the dashboard, or "Send me one now" to see last week's.
59. **Photo descriptions**: each uploaded product photo can have a short description for people using screen readers, typed in the admin product form or suggested by Claude, which looks at the photo ("Suggest", optional, same key). The product page reads it out for the main photo; photos without one use the product's name, as before. Descriptions go with their photos when photos are reordered or removed.
60. **What customers say**: once a product has at least 3 shown reviews with words, a short summary by Claude appears above its reviews, labelled as written by AI. It's rewritten only when the reviews change (a few minutes after, so a burst of edits costs one call), and hiding a review takes it out; below 3 the summary goes. Products already reviewed get theirs on the first sweep. Admins switch it off for the whole store on Admin → Reviews. Optional, same key.
61. **Screening reviews and questions**: Claude checks each new or edited review (with words) and each question for abuse, spam or personal details (phone numbers, addresses), in the background a few a minute, so posting stays instant. Doubtful ones are listed under a new Flagged tab on Admin → Reviews and Admin → Questions, with the reason and a short note, and counted under "Needs attention" on the dashboard; nothing is hidden automatically. "Looks fine" clears a false alarm; hiding works as before. Existing reviews and questions are checked once too. Optional, same key.
62. **Plain-words search**: when a search finds nothing (and there's no typo to fix), Claude reads the words into the store's own filters (search words, a category, brands, a price range, in stock), and the search page offers the result as a link, e.g. "Looking for running shoes, in Fashion, up to ₹3,000? (1 product)", only when it finds something. Each distinct search is remembered for a week, each visitor's network gets 10 new ones an hour and the store 300 a day; admins switch it off on the dashboard, which shows today's use. Optional, same key.
63. **Shopping assistant** (off until an admin switches it on): an "Ask Bazaar" button on shop pages (not the admin panel, checkout or printable documents) opens a chat where Claude helps visitors find products. Claude only reads the catalog, through three tools (search, one product's details, show products), and the products it recommends appear as cards with live prices and stock from the store; it never sees orders or accounts and changes nothing. The panel says it's AI and can make mistakes. The conversation stays in the browser (10 questions a chat) and isn't stored; each network gets 20 messages an hour and the store 500 a day, shown on the dashboard next to the switch. Optional, same key.
64. **Help requests**: "Contact us" at the foot of every page opens a form (a topic and a message; name and email when not logged in), and each order has "Get help with this order". Requests land in Admin → Help (waiting oldest first, then answered and closed); admins are emailed about new ones and follow-ups, reply from the inbox (the customer is emailed), and close or reopen them. Logged-in customers see their requests and the replies on the Help page and can write again. The shopping assistant now points to Contact us. At most 5 new requests an hour per network and 10 open per account.
65. **Download or delete my account** (for India's DPDP Act): on the account page, "Your data" downloads everything the store holds about the customer as a JSON file, and "Close my account" (password, plus a two-step code when that's on) deletes the account, addresses, wishlist, alerts, saved cart, reviews (out of the ratings too), unanswered questions and help requests, and logs them out everywhere. Answered questions stay, signed "A customer"; orders and returns stay for tax records with the name, phone and street removed (city, state and PIN stay for GST). Not while an order is on its way or a return is open, and not for admins. A confirmation email goes to the old address, which can sign up again.
66. **Staff accounts**: roles below admin, given on Admin → Staff to existing accounts. A **packer** sees orders, marks them shipped or delivered (one at a time or in bulk), adds tracking and notes and prints packing slips; **support** sees orders and their notes, answers questions and help requests, and hides or shows reviews. Neither can cancel or refund orders, change products, prices, coupons or settings, export, or see the dashboard; the admin panel shows each only their tabs and the server refuses the rest. Staff get the new-browser login alerts too, and their actions are in the activity log under their names. Admin itself is still only granted with `npm run make-admin`.
67. **Scheduled sales**: on Admin → Sales, a percentage off (1–90%) for a whole category or chosen products, from a start to an end time (up to 31 days). When it starts the prices really drop (rounded down to whole rupees, each variant too; MRP stays, so the discount shows), and when it ends they go back by themselves; admins can end one early or call off one that hasn't started. Cards show a "Sale" tag and product pages "Sale ends in 2 days"; customers watching a product get the usual price-drop email. While a product is on sale its prices can't be edited (stock and the rest can), and it's in one sale at a time. On the free plan a sale starts or ends at the next minute the server is awake.
68. **Lighter product photos**: admins' product photos are shrunk in the browser before uploading (at most 1600 px, a JPEG under 1 MB, like review photos), so full-size phone photos (up to 25 MB) can be picked straight away, and each gets a 400 px small version (≤200 KB). Cards, rows, the cart, wishlist, orders, gallery thumbnails, the assistant's cards and admin lists use the small version; the product page's big photo keeps the full one. Photos from before get theirs with "Make small versions" on Admin → Products; until then the full photo is sent, as before.
69. **Security headers**: every response says which code a page may run and where it may connect (the store itself, plus Razorpay's checkout), that no other site may show the store in a frame, that files are only what they say they are, and that other sites see only the store's address, never the page. Over HTTPS, browsers are told to use only HTTPS for a year. Anything the policy blocks in a visitor's browser is reported to Admin → Errors as "Blocked by the security policy", with the blocked address (just its origin), so a new address Razorpay needs shows up there.
70. **Stock history**: every change to a product's or an option's stock is recorded with why (ordered, cancelled by the customer or the store, an unpaid order released, a return approved, changed by hand, a CSV import, the product added), the order it came from, who made it and the stock after. "Stock history" on a product's edit page lists them, newest first, filterable by option; packers reach it from the items of an order (they can see it, not change products). Kept for a year.
71. **Announcements**: Admin → Announcements schedules a short message (up to 160 characters, optionally linking to a page of the store) across the top of every shop page between two times, e.g. "Orders after 25 Oct ship after Diwali". Visitors can close it (a new one shows again); admins can edit one, take it down early or call it off, and every change is in the activity log. Not shown in the admin panel or on printed invoices.
72. **Store credit**: customers hold credit on their account and spend it at checkout before paying ("Use my store credit", on by default); the rest is paid online or on delivery, and credit can pay for a whole order. It comes from returns (the customer chooses store credit, added the moment the return is approved, or money back the way they paid; money back is never more than was paid in money, the rest comes back as credit), from cancelled orders (the credit part), and from admins, who can give goodwill credit (or take back a mistake) from a help request; the customer is emailed. Credit is a way of paying, so invoices and GST don't change; they say how much was paid with credit. It never expires. The account page shows the balance and every change.
73. **Compare products**: a "Compare" tick under each search and category result and on product pages; up to three products from one category (ticking one from another category starts a new list) gather in a bar at the bottom of the shop, which opens `/compare?slugs=…`: price, rating, brand, highlights, options (sold-out ones marked) and stock side by side, with "Add to cart" or "Choose a size". The list is kept in the browser; the page's address names the products, so a comparison can be shared. Prices and stock are live, and hidden products drop out.
74. **Customers page**: Admin → Customers lists every account with its orders, money spent (orders placed, shipped or delivered, less refunds), last order and store credit, searchable by name or email and sortable by newest, most spent or latest order. Each customer has a page with their details and addresses, totals, latest orders, returns, help requests and store credit history, where an admin can also give store credit. Customer names on admin order pages and help requests link to it.
75. **Reorder suggestions**: "Running out soon" on the dashboard lists products and options whose stock lasts 14 days or less at the last 30 days' sales (or since a newer product was added, at least a week), soonest first, with what's left, sales per day and about how many to order to last a month; each links to its stock history. The morning low-stock email includes the same list, and now also goes out when nothing is at 5 or fewer but something is running out.
76. **Search insights**: Admin → Searches shows, per week (Monday to Sunday, India time), how many searches shoppers made, the most searched words with how many products each found, and the searches that found nothing, each linked to the shop's results. Only plain searches count (paging, sorting and filters don't count again), by their words only, with nothing about who searched; kept six months.
77. **Gift cards**: logged-in customers buy a ₹500, ₹1,000, ₹2,000 or ₹5,000 gift card on `/gift-cards` (footer link), paid online through Razorpay, for someone by email: sent as soon as it's paid or at 9 am on a day they choose up to 90 days ahead, with a message; the buyer gets a receipt with a copy of the code.
